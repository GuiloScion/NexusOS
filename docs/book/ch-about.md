# About the author

**Noah Parsons** wrote NexusOS, the reference kernel this book is built around, and now this book about it. He works on systems software, computational physics, and applied policy research from Newcastle, Wyoming.

Alongside NexusOS he maintains **MechanicsDSL**, a physics compiler that translates LaTeX-inspired notation into executable simulations across a dozen code-generation backends (C++, CUDA, OpenMP, Rust, Julia, Fortran, MATLAB, JavaScript, WebAssembly, Arduino, Unity, Modelica). It is open source under MIT (Zenodo DOI [10.5281/zenodo.17771040](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17771040)) and has been downloaded around 13,000 times across 67 countries at the time of writing, with institutional mirrors on bandersnatch, Nexus, and devpi. He also works on analytical modeling of energy transfer and information flow in boundary-driven nonequilibrium quantum spin chains, a piece currently in preparation toward submission.

His policy research, conducted in 2025 as Director of Civic Innovation at the American Forge Institute (since dissolved), has appeared in the peer-reviewed *Applied Journal of Economics, Law and Governance* (a 32-page paper on grid modernization, Vol 1, Issue 2) and in shorter Zenodo-archived briefs on Medicare drug-price negotiation, the October 2025 federal government shutdown, and U.S. industrial competitiveness.
